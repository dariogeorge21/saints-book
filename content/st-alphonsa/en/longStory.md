# The Sacred Life of St. Alphonsa

## Early Life & Divine Calling
Annakutty Muttathupadathu was born on 19 August 1910 in Kudamalur, Kerala, into a devout Syro-Malabar Catholic family. Having lost her mother in infancy, she was raised first by her grandparents and later by her maternal aunt. The devout family atmosphere, daily prayers, and acts of charity she witnessed in childhood nurtured her early desire to belong completely to God.

## Consecration to Christ
As Annakutty grew, relatives wished to arrange an advantageous marriage for her, but she remained steadfast in her longing for religious life. To preserve her commitment to Christ, she endured significant family opposition and intense physical trials. With the counsel of her spiritual director, Father James Muricken, she joined the Franciscan Clarist Congregation at Bharananganam on 24 May 1927.

## Religious Life and Formation
She received the religious habit on 19 May 1930, adopting the name Alphonsa of the Immaculate Conception. Whenever her fragile health allowed, she taught young schoolchildren at Vakakkad, served as a parish catechist, and assisted with convent administration. Those around her quickly recognised her humility, gentleness, and deep love for students.

## Severe Illness and Patient Suffering
Alphonsa’s religious journey was marked by recurrent and agonizing illness. In 1935, while severely ill during her novitiate, she experienced a remarkable recovery attributed to the intercession of Father Kuriakose Elias Chavara. She made her perpetual profession on 12 August 1936. Subsequent years brought typhoid, double pneumonia, and a painful tumour, yet she offered all her suffering as a spiritual oblation of love.

## Canonisation and Sacred Legacy
St. Alphonsa passed into eternal life on 28 July 1946 at the age of 35 in Bharananganam. Pope John Paul II beatified her at Kottayam on 8 February 1986, and Pope Benedict XVI canonised her on 12 October 2008 in Rome, making her India's first female Catholic saint. Her shrine in Bharananganam draws thousands of pilgrims seeking solace and healing.
