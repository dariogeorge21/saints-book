# St. Alphonsa — Short Story

St. Alphonsa of the Immaculate Conception (1910–1946) was an Indian religious sister and the first woman of Indian origin to be canonised as a saint by the Catholic Church. Born Annakutty Muttathupadathu in Kudamalur, Kerala, she joined the Franciscan Clarist Congregation at Bharananganam, dedicating her brief life to prayer, teaching, and compassionate service.

Despite enduring prolonged physical suffering, persistent illnesses, and painful trials throughout her young adulthood, St. Alphonsa bore her tribulations with extraordinary patience, joy, and serene faith in Christ. Canonised by Pope Benedict XVI on October 12, 2008, her feast day is celebrated on July 28, and she remains a cherished patron of the sick and all who suffer.
