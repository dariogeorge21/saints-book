# The Sacred Life of St. Euphrasia

## Early Life & Marian Devotion
Rose Eluvathingal was born on 17 October 1877 in Kattoor, Kerala, into the devout family of Anthony and Kunjethy. Her mother nurtured in her a tender love for the Blessed Virgin Mary and recounted stories of early Christian saints. At the age of nine, following a personal interior vision of Mary, Rose made a solemn vow to consecrate herself exclusively to God.

## Overcoming Obstacles to Consecration
Her father initially opposed her desire to become a religious sister, wishing her instead to enter an arranged marriage. Rose persevered with patient prayers, fasting, and unwavering trust. Following the sudden death of her younger sister, her father had a change of heart and personally accompanied her to the Congregation of the Mother of Carmel at Koonammavu.

## Carmelite Formation and Perpetual Vows
She entered the convent as a postulant on 10 May 1897 and received the religious name Euphrasia of the Sacred Heart of Jesus. She received the Carmelite habit on 10 January 1898. Though afflicted by bouts of severe, life-threatening sickness, her health was restored, and on 24 May 1900 she made her perpetual vows during the foundation of St. Mary's Convent in Ollur.

## The Praying Mother and the Mobile Tabernacle
Sister Euphrasia served as mistress of novices from 1904 to 1913, nurturing young sisters with wisdom and humility. She later served as mother superior from 1913 to 1916. She spent nearly 48 years at the Ollur convent. Her hours before the Blessed Sacrament and devotion to the Rosary led the faithful to call her the "Praying Mother," while her sisters affectionately named her the "Mobile Tabernacle."

## Canonisation and Spiritual Heritage
St. Euphrasia died quietly at Ollur on 29 August 1952 at the age of 74. Pope Benedict XVI beatified her on 3 December 2006, and Pope Francis canonised her at St. Peter's Square in Rome on 23 November 2014. Her shrine in Ollur remains a sanctuary of prayer, healing, and peace for all who seek her intercession.
