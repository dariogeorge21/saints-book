# The Sacred Life of St. Francis of Assisi

## Early Life and Youthful Ambition
Francis was born in Assisi, central Italy, around 1181 or 1182, into the prosperous merchant family of Pietro di Bernardone and Pica. Baptised Giovanni, he was called Francesco by his father. As a young man, he revelled in fine clothing, feasts, and poetry, dreaming of knighthood and martial glory while his parents envisioned him taking over their thriving cloth business.

## Conversion and the Call to Rebuild
During conflict between Assisi and Perugia in 1202, Francis was captured and imprisoned for a year. A severe illness turned his mind toward spiritual matters. In 1205, near the crumbling church of San Damiano, he heard Christ speak from the crucifix: "Francis, go and repair my house, which as you see is falling into ruin." Renouncing all family inheritance before the Bishop of Assisi, Francis clothed himself in a rough peasant tunic and devoted himself to the care of lepers and the restoration of sacred spaces.

## Founding the Franciscan Order
Companions gathered around Francis, seeking to live solely according to the holy Gospel. In 1209, Pope Innocent III gave oral approval to the Order of Friars Minor. Clare of Assisi joined in 1212 to establish the Poor Clares, followed by a Third Order for laypeople. Francis traveled tirelessly across Europe and even journeyed to Egypt during the Fifth Crusade in 1219 to meet Sultan al-Kamil in an extraordinary dialogue of peace.

## The Stigmata, Canticle, and Passing
In 1224, while in deep contemplative prayer on Mount La Verna, Francis received the stigmata, the holy wounds of Christ. In failing health and near-blindness, he composed the luminous Canticle of the Creatures, praising God through Brother Sun, Sister Moon, and all creation. He died near Assisi on 3 October 1226 and was canonised by Pope Gregory IX on 16 July 1228.
